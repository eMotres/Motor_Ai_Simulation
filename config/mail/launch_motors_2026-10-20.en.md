## Why AeroStator

We build electric motors and generators. AeroStator is the engineering platform behind that work, and its software is open source (Apache-2.0). Every motor in it has a passport computed by finite-element (FEM) simulation. You can tune it instantly in **Configure** and see the effect on torque, losses, temperature and efficiency. You can also check how it pulls a real propeller, and look at it in 3-D.

The goal is to go **from idea to finished product together**: a motor, its controller and propeller, and in the end a whole drone, robot joint or vehicle, simulated as one system.

## How you can take part

- **Use the motors** in your own designs, and tell us what is missing. The assistant on the site turns your question into a ticket.
- **Co-develop modules** with us and other engineers: a propeller, a gearbox, a controller. We review and integrate good work.
- **Bring your AI agent.** AeroStator speaks MCP, so your agent can browse the catalogue, check whether a motor fits your requirements and run simulations for you. Endpoint: https://aerostator.com/mcp
- **Bring your own compute** for heavy simulations, if you have a server.

## Motors you can work with now

- **CIANO14 40, L12 and L20** (40 mm, 12 slots / 14 poles, propeller drives). **Both are already in production.** We expect the first motors to be available **within about a month**.
- **CIANO14 50, L15** (50 mm, propeller drives).
- **CIANO28 85, L15** (85 mm, frameless kit for robot joints).

Each has its rated and peak points, propeller load charts, losses, temperatures and a 3-D model.
