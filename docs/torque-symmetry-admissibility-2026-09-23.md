# Admissible sector symmetry for torque comparisons — 2026-09-23

The saved 30 mm motor has **12 slots and 14 poles**, with the standard single-layer 12s/14p winding and `n_sectors=2`. Its admissible solver requests are full (`NS=1`) and half (`NS=2`), **not quarter**. The guard `geometry_2d.validate_sector_symmetry` first requires both slot and pole counts divisible by `NS`; 14 is not divisible by four. It also requires the resolved phase and direction at slot `s+slots/NS` to match the original phase with sign `(-1)^(poles/NS)`, and the paired-stator CAD family must have an even number of slots per sector. The FEM entry calls this guard before calibration/mesh (`fem_solver_2d.py` around lines 3101–3112). For 12s/14p, the half-sector spans six slots and seven poles, so its scalar field boundary is **antiperiodic** and the winding directions across 180° must reverse. The full disk has no sector boundary. A quarter has 3.5 poles and cannot represent a repeat or sign-reversed pole pattern at 90°. Lowering the sector count for speed cannot override that physics.

An existing **24-slot/28-pole** fixture legitimately supports full, half and quarter comparisons. `tests/test_masses.py` defines the explicit 150 mm/35 mm `G150` geometry with 24 slots, 28 poles, four six-slot/seven-pole segments and pinned materials; `tests/test_sector_symmetry_guard.py` independently accepts its standard generated single- and double-layer layouts at `NS=1,2,4`. For this fixture the half wedge has 12 slots and 14 poles, hence **periodic** boundary sign `+1`; the quarter wedge has six slots and seven poles, hence **antiperiodic** sign `−1`. The solver computes the sign from `poles_per_sector` at `fem_solver_2d.py` around lines 3115–3124. A custom winding can invalidate a geometrically divisible sector: `tests/test_sector_symmetry_guard.py` proves that a balanced but asymmetric 24s/28p layout is rejected for both half and quarter. Material cards, magnet pattern, geometry exclusions and excitation must also repeat with the stated sign; count divisibility and a generated layout are necessary checks, not proof that an arbitrary customized machine is sector-equivalent.

A minimal future comparison should freeze the 24s/28p `G150` geometry, winding, material cards, phase-current vector and speed, and choose one **common physical rotor-angle grid** snapped to all three slip lattices. Hold the intended full-circumference slip-node density, element order, nominal element size, gap-layer count and accepted nonlinear residual target fixed; record the actual meshes, DOF counts, snapped angles, slip shifts, phase currents and convergence paths. Compare unfiltered Maxwell-stress torque and the same reconstructed fixed-current virtual-work functional at matching physical angles, scaling each wedge by its sector count. Include a small mesh/angular-step sensitivity check before drawing a numerical conclusion. Sector-count equality alone is not independent validation: the solver's full-ring path can stitch rotated half-mesh copies (`fem_solver_2d.py` around lines 8112–8189), while a sector mesh may have different boundary elements and weld interpolation; sector multiplication and harmonic cancellation can make reported means look equal even when local fields or ripple differ. Periodic or antiperiodic boundary conditions also suppress modes incompatible with the wedge, so agreement of a filtered mean must not be mistaken for preservation of physical harmonics.
