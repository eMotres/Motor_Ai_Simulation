# Propeller S1: current-drive versus voltage feasibility

**Parent correction during mapper implementation:** delta target/readback conventions conflict across source comments and labels (`simulation.py:7770-7774`, `fem_solver_2d.py:12999-13002`, coupled final-pass target comment versus `_point_error_pct`). The unconditional delta conversion interpretation below is not accepted qualification evidence. Until independently verified, a delta electrical gate must remain unknown even for apparently √3-consistent fixture numbers. The supported successful mapper is star-only, where winding and terminal RMS coincide. No solver convention was changed and no delta physical validation was performed.

**Read-only physics-policy audit, 2026-10-07.** This note distinguishes motor-only continuous capability from a controller-and-pack system rating. It changes no solver or qualification code and certifies no operating point.

## Finding

A separate motor current-capability number should **not** be a prerequisite for a motor-only S1 result when the requested S1 current is the operating point being solved and the same-point thermal limits are fully judged. In this product's Simulation schema, `simulation.max_current` / `simulation.current_a` are solve inputs, not a motor ampacity (`routes/simulation.py:2362, 266-277, 431-446`; the earlier audit, `docs/propeller-s1-physical-limit-sources-2026-10-07.md`, “Current-limit source audit”). Treating those inputs as a rating is invalid. The thermal point and continuous-temperature policy should bound motor current.

That does **not** make a current-driven solve evidence that the selected electrical source can sustain the point. The solved terminal-voltage fundamental is explicitly a *seed*, because a nonlinear voltage-driven solve can land at a different current (`simulation/postproc.py:216-252`). Therefore the current-only private bridge can establish a motor torque/thermal candidate, but not voltage-feasible motor operation. Absence of a separate motor ampacity must not be confused with proof of electrical feasibility.

## Supported same-point electrical evidence

For a motor-only rating at a declared ideal DC link, the existing model supports a voltage-feasibility check without inventing a current rating: run the unchanged coupled PWM path at the candidate RPM, with the candidate phase-current target, the explicitly resolved DC bus and modulation policy, and evaluate the returned *same run*. `_inverter_settings()` resolves the bus, modulation, connection-specific fundamental ceiling and preserves the requested current target (`routes/coupled.py:738-927`). The coupled PWM regulator re-aims voltage to that target (`:2611-2698`), while `_inverter_record()` reports the resolved bus/modulation, actual solved current, target error/`on_point`, and DC convergence state (`:3780-3868`). Qualification still requires the full run's solver, torque, thermal, and any applicable mechanical gates; PWM target success alone is not a motor S1 result.

The voltage/current coordinates must remain explicit. Coupled output reports `P_shaft_net_W` as delivered rotor-shaft power after bearing/windage loss (`routes/simulation.py:7268-7285`) and `I_winding_rms_A` with the connection name. The star case is accepted by the current mapper because the reported terminal-phase and winding RMS coordinates coincide. The delta conversion is unresolved: source comments and labels disagree across `routes/simulation.py:7770-7780`, `simulation/fem_solver_2d.py:12999-13002`, and coupled PWM target/readback code. Do not apply a √3 conversion or compare delta current against a limit until the actual circuit/current convention is independently verified. Delta electrical evidence therefore stays unknown, including when fixture numbers appear to fit a proposed conversion. A point mapper should use the actual PWM solved-current/on-point evidence and exact frozen connection; the current-drive fundamental-voltage seed is only a pre-screen, not a substitute.

For a **controller-limited system** S1 result, retain the same-point PWM solve and additionally bind a real selected Controller and its configured parallel count. `configure_limits.current_limit()` derives an inverter-side phase RMS ceiling from that device's continuous drain-current card rating at 100 °C case temperature (`configure_limits.py:21-80`); coupled inverter-loss output reports junction temperature and the device's `t_j_max_c` (`inverter/losses.py:1718-1730`). These limits belong to the controller/system rating, not the motor-only rating. Missing controller identity/rating means the system-current gate is unknown; it does not invalidate a separately labelled motor-only thermal rating.

## Source and pack boundary

The coupled PWM route has a named bus source: request, Controller manual `v_dc_V`, configuration battery, loaded-machine battery, or legacy record (`inverter/drive_source.py:264-318`; coupled source resolution at `routes/coupled.py:738-807, 4404-4415`). When battery `v_nom` exists it wins; `v_min` is used only if nominal is absent (and with both min/max but no nominal, the resolver uses their midpoint). The coupled inverter result records that resolved fixed DC bus and its provenance (`routes/coupled.py:916-927`). Thus a result can state feasibility **at that resolved bus**.

Do not upgrade this into a loaded-pack guarantee from `v_min`, `i_charge_max_a`, or `r_int_mohm`: the coupled PWM source resolver selects a fixed voltage and the inspected coupled route does not apply the Simulation route's iterative loaded-battery fixed point. The latter is a distinct path in `routes/simulation.py:3900-3958, 4640-4665, 4870-4881`. A battery/system S1 claim needs a supported same-point pack voltage/current/sag result; until then, label the motor point “feasible at resolved fixed bus” or leave loaded-pack feasibility unknown.

The ideal `drive="pwm"` mode resolves its modulation as sine; a saved Controller mode resolves that Controller's selected modulation. The ceiling is computed from bus, winding connection, and modulation policy (`routes/coupled.py:858-911`; PWM definitions in `simulation/pwm.py`). Do not substitute an assumed sine/SVPWM factor or an unrelated saved device. Require the actual run to remain inside its reported ceiling and its reported PWM/DC convergence conditions.

## Minimal contract correction

The point adapter may keep `drive_current_limit_a` as an optional sourced **controller/system** cap when one exists, but it should not require it for motor-only rating. Its present “missing ceiling ⇒ electrical unknown” behavior is conservative, not proof that the motor cannot be rated. Replace that motor-only electrical prerequisite with explicit, same-point voltage-source feasibility evidence from the voltage-fed coupled run. Keep a separate controller-limit gate for a controller/system claim, and a separate loaded-pack gate where the product claim requires battery sag/current limits. Search bounds and requested current never become equipment ratings.

Required evidence for a motor-only electrical gate: exact build and winding connection; named resolved fixed bus and modulation source; candidate RPM/current target; actual voltage-fed coupled response with actual solved winding/phase current, target error/on-point, no over-ceiling refusal, and the route's DC/PWM convergence flags; same-point delivered torque and fully judged thermal record. Any missing, mismatched, or nonconverged evidence remains unknown. For controller/system qualification, append the matching card's current ceiling and junction-temperature result. For pack qualification, require an actual supported loaded-pack result rather than deriving one from the nominal bus.

This is a supported qualification contract, not a claim that the current point adapter or private bridge already performs the voltage-fed check. The inspected bridge currently rejects non-current drive (`coupled_propeller_s1_private_bridge.py:241-244`), so no result from that path should be marked voltage-feasible until that path is implemented and tested. Keep publication qualification false in the meantime.
